How the tool works

  1. Site — the map shows all of downtown; click any lot or search an address. The study area around it (the circle) is built from OpenStreetMap streets, sidewalks, crossings, signals and stops, plus every neighbouring building with an estimated floor area and use. Sliders set how busy the surroundings are.
  2. Building — the massing as blocks with a use (or an imported Downtown Massing Tool project), the programme it implies, the entrances, parking ramp, loading and pick-up kerb, and the public realm: places to stay and through-block walkways.
  3. People — who the programme brings: eight user groups with speed, schedule, stairs, transit and car habits and how long they stop; and who comes with each use.
  4. Run (top bar) — pick a time window and run: the lot as it is today and with the project, same random seed. Watch animates one scenario. Pedestrians walk the sidewalk graph and wait at crossings; vehicles queue at signals and spill back; trucks and ride-hail stop at the kerb.
  5. Results — levels of service for sidewalks, junctions and links before and after; who is on the site; walksheds; change map; tables, CSV and a printable report.

Everything is an estimate for design comparison, not a traffic impact assessment. Calibrate the rates and background levels against counts when you have them.
